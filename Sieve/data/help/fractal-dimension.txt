index: fractal dimension, fractals, FRACTALS line, rulesets, Fibonacci word, golden ratio, L-system, substitution, Koch, dragon curve, index book, spider web, Penrose tiling, Pythagoras tree, box-counting dimension, IFS, Barnsley fern, Mandelbrot set, Julia set, Newton fractal

The fractal dimension is a line of the hallway, its door after AI, in light purple. Its books are every ruleset there is, from the shortest, each at an address of its own; the fractals charted by hand stand among them, named. Its first book is the index of the charted ones, saying where each stands; starting on the line, you start at it (the line is far too long to walk back to it from halfway along, where the other lines start), and {key1:hallway.anywhere/home|Home} goes back to it.

## Walking it
Go left through AI's far door, or choose FRACTALS as the line to start on. Hover over a book to see its name; take it with {key1:hallway.anywhere/take|E}, as any item. Warp ({key1:hallway.anywhere/warp|T}) to a fractal by its id, such as dragon-curve, or "index" for the first book.

## A fractal in hand
It shows what it is, its picture at the depth you hold it at, and its ruleset. {key1:hallway.holding/next_page|N} or {key1:hallway.anywhere/zoom_in|=} grows it a step deeper; {key1:hallway.holding/previous_page|B} or {key1:hallway.anywhere/zoom_out|-} takes it a step back. {key1:hallway.holding/view|Z} opens it in the viewer, large, where N and B step it too; {key1:hallway.holding/save|F} saves its ruleset, the steps that make it (the picture is only how it is shown); {key1:hallway.holding/favourite|H} keeps it as a favourite.

## The kinds of ruleset
- A substitution: at every step each symbol becomes its word (0 becomes 01, 1 becomes 0, for the Fibonacci word). Drawn as its words one under another, each stretched across, so the pattern is seen repeating at every scale.
- An L-system: drawn as a turtle draws. F and G draw a line forward, f moves without one, + and - turn by its angle, [ and ] keep a place and come back to it.
- Widened (sieve-fractal-v2): an L-system whose angle has a tenth of a degree (22.5, as many plants are drawn), or whose step is scaled: with "scale 0.707", " makes the step from there on 0.707 times as long and ' as much longer, so spirals, trees that thin and spider webs can be written. The whole space holds these too, after the others.
- Widened again (sieve-fractal-v3): angles as a fraction of a turn (360/34) or with up to six decimals; ! swaps what + and - turn; digits defined as turns by stated degrees or as other step ratios; several rules for one symbol, one picked at each place from the seed, so a bush grows a little differently with each seed but the same every time with one; rules that apply only beside a given symbol (contexts); & ^ \ and / turning out of the page, so a plant or a Hilbert curve is grown in three dimensions and drawn seen from one side; words up to 255 symbols. After v2's in the whole space.
- Beyond rewriting (sieve-fractal-v4): an iterated function system, maps of the plane each shrinking the figure into a part of itself (Barnsley's fern is four), drawn as the points every sequence of maps reaches, each coloured by the map that placed it, N taking it a level deeper; or an escape-time or convergence formula, z's next value written with its operator first (+ ^ z 2 c is z squared plus c: the Mandelbrot set), drawn point by point, each coloured by how soon it escaped, or by where it settled (Newton's method), N following each point for twice as many steps. A julia line fixes c and starts z at the point (a Julia set); a view line sets the window. After v3's in the whole space.
- Cellular automata (sieve-fractal-v5), a family of this dimension rather than one of their own: an elementary automaton, one of Wolfram's rules 0 to 255 over a row of cells, drawn as its rows one under another (rule 90 from one cell is Sierpinski's triangle), or a life-like one such as Conway's Game of Life (B3/S23: a dead cell with three live neighbours comes alive, a live one with two or three stays), drawn as its field. Each depth is a generation: N takes it one on, so depth is time, for every family here (sieve fractal draw --all-depths writes each depth as a frame).
- Followed as a path or grown at random (sieve-fractal-v6): a strange attractor, a map of the plane moving a point again and again (Henon's, x to 1 - 1.4 x squared + y and y to 0.3 x), drawn as the points it visits; a flow, three equations saying how fast x, y and z change (Lorenz's butterfly), followed a small step at a time and drawn as its path seen from the side; or an aggregate, particles wandering at random until they stick to the cluster, drawn as its branches. Each depth doubles the points, steps or particles.

## The known fractals
On the setup page, the FRACTALS rows: the longest ruleset on the line (44 to 1024 characters, 256 at first, so every charted one but four is on it: Voderberg's tiling, 590, Fractint's Koch3, 272, the Sierpinski carpet as an IFS, 331, and Fractint's name in 22 maps, 690; the longer, the longer it takes to count: about 4 seconds at 512), and the known fractals: include (every ruleset, the charted ones named where they stand), exclude (every ruleset but the charted ones, and no index: warp to a charted one says so, and where to change it) or only (the charted ones alone, after the index). Most rulesets draw little or nothing; the charted ones are the landmarks.

## Filtering the noise
The magnifying glass beside FRACTALS on the setup page opens its filters: draws something (a line, or two colours), grows (longer at every depth), no idle rules (none for a symbol never met, none rewriting a symbol to itself), canonical (one ruleset of each renaming of its symbols, and an IFS's maps in order), and fractal dimension (a drawing whose box-counting dimension, measured, is at least the least you set: 1.10 at first, which sets aside the straight lines and curves hardly bent; the Koch curve measures 1.29, the Sierpinski triangle 1.58; escape and convergence pictures are not measured and pass), strange (an attractor or a flow on which two starts a billionth apart end far apart, as a chaotic one does; one that settles on a point or a loop, or flies off, does not), and lasts (an automaton still alive and changing at the generation you set, 32 at first; one that dies out, freezes, repeats or is a glider moving on does not). The first four are counted exactly on every ruleset of sieve-fractal-v1 to v3, nearly all of the line, so the setup page says how much of the line they keep (with all four, 10^-59.27); the other three judge each ruleset by drawing or running it, on the JUDGE-ONLY FILTERS tab. Ticked, a ruleset that fails is marked or hidden, as on any line; the index and the known fractals are never judged. sieve filters --line fractals --sample 1000 says how many of 1,000 rulesets across the space each passes.

## In compact
With the mode set to compact and any of the first four ticked, the line holds only what they keep, numbered without gaps: the index, then the v1 to v3 rulesets that pass, then the v4 to v6 ones, which those four judge by drawing or running each and so cannot number. With all four ticked, an address on it is 196.89 bits shorter than on the whole line (of 1,345.7). The survivors are in an order of their own, not the line's: by the set of symbols the axiom reaches, then the order it reaches them in. A known fractal the four set aside has no place on it, and warping to one says so (the dragon curve, whose X and Y are a renaming of A and B, is one). The other three, and the four on v4 to v6, hide what they set aside, as hide does.

## In other lines
The third FRACTALS row, in other lines, judges the image line by the fractals: include keeps only the pictures that are a charted fractal drawn (at any depth, fitted as a picture warped in is), exclude sets those aside, off leaves the image line alone. It is the image line's fractal-drawn filter, ticked or not, so the image line's magnifying glass shows it too. Each picture counts turned and mirrored (eight ways) and inverted too, so with the image line compact its shelves hold just the fractals drawn: 62,356 of them at 10 x 10 in black and white (5,279 as drawn). The filter's colours setting can also take every colouring of each picture's shape (on 16 colours, 12,830,096,076,621,376 pictures, counted exactly but judged, so the line marks or hides them rather than compacting). Depth being time, the same row sets the video line's fractal-moving filter: a video that is a charted fractal growing, its frames its depths in turn (an automaton's generations, a fern's levels), from depth 0 to 32 on, turned and coloured as the pictures are; at 10 x 10 and 4 frames in black and white, 23,830 of them. And the models line's fractal-modelled filter: a model that is a charted fractal's lines at a depth, each line a face naming its two ends, in each of the 24 turns of a cube; at the line's 8 vertices and 12 faces, 3,216 of them (only the shallow depths fit). A line is a degenerate face, so the models line's distinct-indices filter sets them all aside already. The pages line's fractal-written filter: a page that is a charted fractal's word at a depth (531 pages of 32 characters on ascii96; on lower27 only the words its letters can write, 43). And the audio line's fractal-scored filter: a melody made from a charted fractal as Prusinkiewicz made scores of L-systems, each line across a note, its height the pitch and its length the note's (3,548 melodies of 16 notes, at every pitch).

## On the command line
sieve fractal lists the charted fractals, each with its measured dimension and where it is from; sieve fractal show and draw grow one to a depth and draw it as a picture; sieve fractal count, at and index count the whole space, give the ruleset at an address, and the address of one; with --compact and a filters file, at and index do the same on the line in compact.
