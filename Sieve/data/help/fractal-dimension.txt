index: fractal dimension, fractals, FRACTALS line, rulesets, Fibonacci word, golden ratio, L-system, substitution, Koch, dragon curve, index book, spider web, Penrose tiling, Pythagoras tree, box-counting dimension, IFS, Barnsley fern, Mandelbrot set, Julia set, Newton fractal

The fractal dimension is a line of the hallway, its door after AI, in light purple. Its books are every ruleset there is, from the shortest, each at an address of its own; the fractals charted by hand stand among them, named. Its first book is the index of the charted ones, saying where each stands.

## Walking it
Go left through AI's far door, or choose FRACTALS as the line to start on. Hover over a book to see its name; take it with {key1:hallway.anywhere/take|E}, as any item. Warp ({key1:hallway.anywhere/warp|T}) to a fractal by its id, such as dragon-curve, or "index" for the first book.

## A fractal in hand
It shows what it is, its picture at the depth you hold it at, and its ruleset. {key1:hallway.holding/next_page|N} or {key1:hallway.anywhere/zoom_in|=} grows it a step deeper; {key1:hallway.holding/previous_page|B} or {key1:hallway.anywhere/zoom_out|-} takes it a step back. {key1:hallway.holding/view|Z} opens it in the viewer, large, where N and B step it too; {key1:hallway.holding/save|F} saves its ruleset, the steps that make it (the picture is only how it is shown); {key1:hallway.holding/favourite|H} keeps it as a favourite.

## The kinds of ruleset
- A substitution: at every step each symbol becomes its word (0 becomes 01, 1 becomes 0, for the Fibonacci word). Drawn as its words one under another, each stretched across, so the pattern is seen repeating at every scale.
- An L-system: drawn as a turtle draws. F and G draw a line forward, f moves without one, + and - turn by its angle, [ and ] keep a place and come back to it.
- Widened (sieve-fractal-v2): an L-system whose angle has a tenth of a degree (22.5, as many plants are drawn), or whose step is scaled: with "scale 0.707", " makes the step from there on 0.707 times as long and ' as much longer, so spirals, trees that thin and spider webs can be written. The whole space holds these too, after the others.
- Widened again (sieve-fractal-v3): angles as a fraction of a turn (360/34) or with up to six decimals; ! swaps what + and - turn; digits defined as turns by stated degrees or as other step ratios; several rules for one symbol, one picked at each place from the seed, so a bush grows a little differently with each seed but the same every time with one; rules that apply only beside a given symbol (contexts); & ^ \ and / turning out of the page, so a plant or a Hilbert curve is grown in three dimensions and drawn seen from one side; words up to 255 symbols. After v2's in the whole space.
- Beyond rewriting (sieve-fractal-v4): an iterated function system, maps of the plane each shrinking the figure into a part of itself (Barnsley's fern is four), drawn as the points every sequence of maps reaches, each coloured by the map that placed it, N taking it a level deeper; or an escape-time or convergence formula, z's next value written with its operator first (+ ^ z 2 c is z squared plus c: the Mandelbrot set), drawn point by point, each coloured by how soon it escaped, or by where it settled (Newton's method), N following each point for twice as many steps. A julia line fixes c and starts z at the point (a Julia set); a view line sets the window. After v3's in the whole space.

## The known fractals
On the setup page, the FRACTALS rows: the longest ruleset on the line (44 to 1024 characters, 256 at first, so every charted one but four is on it: Voderberg's tiling, 590, Fractint's Koch3, 272, the Sierpinski carpet as an IFS, 331, and Fractint's name in 22 maps, 690; the longer, the longer it takes to count: about 4 seconds at 512), and the known fractals: include (every ruleset, the charted ones named where they stand), exclude (every ruleset but the charted ones, and no index: warp to a charted one says so, and where to change it) or only (the charted ones alone, after the index). Most rulesets draw little or nothing; the charted ones are the landmarks.

## Filtering the noise
The magnifying glass beside FRACTALS on the setup page opens its filters, on the JUDGE-ONLY FILTERS tab: draws something (a line, or two colours), grows (longer at every depth), no idle rules (none for a symbol never met, none rewriting a symbol to itself), canonical (one ruleset of each renaming of its symbols, and an IFS's maps in order), and fractal dimension (a drawing whose box-counting dimension, measured, is at least the least you set: 1.10 at first, which sets aside the straight lines and curves hardly bent; the Koch curve measures 1.29, the Sierpinski triangle 1.58; escape and convergence pictures are not measured and pass). Ticked, a ruleset that fails is marked or hidden, as on any line; the index and the known fractals are never judged. sieve filters --line fractals --sample 1000 says how many of 1,000 rulesets across the space each passes.

## In other lines
The third FRACTALS row, in other lines, judges the image line by the fractals: include keeps only the pictures that are a charted fractal drawn (at any depth, fitted as a picture warped in is), exclude sets those aside, off leaves the image line alone. It is the image line's fractal-drawn filter, ticked or not, so the image line's magnifying glass shows it too. With the image line compact, its shelves hold just the fractals drawn: 1,057 of them at 10 x 10 in black and white.

## The charted fractals
The index, then 155 fractals, each with where it was transcribed from (its hover and page say "after ..."): the Fibonacci word (the golden ratio in two symbols), the Thue-Morse sequence, the Cantor set, the Koch curve and snowflake, Sierpinski's triangle, the dragon curve, the Hilbert, Levy C and Gosper curves and a fractal plant; then the Koch islands and variants and the plants of The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants (Prusinkiewicz and Lindenmayer), the Sierpinski, Moore and Peano curves, the twindragon and terdragon, Mandelbrot's islands and curves and Penrose's tilings as Fractint's files write them, the period-doubling, Rudin-Shapiro, paperfolding, Baum-Sweet, tribonacci and Pell words, and three written for Sieve: an orb web, the Pythagoras tree and the H tree; then those v3 can write: the Cesaro and double Cesaro curves, Mandelbrot's snowflake sweeps, the pentagram, Voderberg's tiling and the Vertigo spirals, more Penrose tilings, Fractint's other plants, trees and curves, the 5 x 5 FASS curve, the Hilbert curve, a bush and a flowering plant in three dimensions, stochastic plants and Hogeweg and Hesper's trees grown by contexts; then those v4 can write: Barnsley's fern and a mutated one, Fractint's IFS (coral, crystal, a dragon, a spiral, a tree and Fractint's own name among them), the dragons, the Levy C curve, the Cantor set, Sierpinski's triangle and carpet, Vicsek's cross and Koch's curve as IFS, the Mandelbrot set and its Multibrots, the Burning Ship and the tricorn, Julia sets (Douady's rabbit among them), the lambda and magnet fractals, Newton's method for z^3 - 1 and z^4 - 1, and the Collatz fractal. More are charted by adding them to fractals/chart.txt beside the program, each as its own .fractal file with a source line after it.

## On the command line
sieve fractal lists the charted fractals, each with its measured dimension and where it is from; sieve fractal show and draw grow one to a depth and draw it as a picture; sieve fractal count, at and index count the whole space, give the ruleset at an address, and the address of one.
