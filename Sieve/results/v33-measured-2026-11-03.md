# v33 measured, two packer faults fixed, and installing made quicker (3 November 2026)

Edward: "go ahead with the measurements next Then run a full code review and optimisation pass to
see if we can make any more gains in decoding time speeds". Measured on a Linux machine of 4
logical processors (16 GB of memory, the kernel giving huge pages where a program asks for them).
The sizes are the same on any machine; the times are this machine's.

## The run

`tools/bench/bench_files.py` on enwik8, Silesia's twelve files, Don Quixote (`pg996.txt`) and
`control.py`, two ways each: for size with Try every way (`size/try`) and for decoding without it
(`decode/no`). Every `.sieve` was installed again and checked.

It found two faults, both in the packer (no change to the format). Each is below; the figures
after them are from this build, with both fixed.

## Installing: the review and what was made quicker

Where installing spends its time: the x86 and lzma2 streams decoded, then the listing read (each
file taken from its stream, or queued), then the queued files decoded at once on every core
(`decode_deferred`, under a memory budget), then groups cut, files remade, deltas, masks and copies
applied, every file checked against its SHA-256. Mixing decodes as slowly as it codes, so a mixed
file's own time is the floor; what could be won was the order around it.

1. **Files made again from a recipe, on every core.** A PNG re-saved, a JPEG from its brunsli
   form, an archive remade from what it holds: each was made one at a time while the listing was
   read, though each needs only its own bytes. They are now jobs for the same pool as the mixed
   files (each costed at twice its bytes in and out against the memory budget), checked in the
   listing's order after. The remakers were already safe on several threads (the packer runs them
   so; stb's writer is behind its lock). Measured on the repository's 95 pictures (89 PNGs and 6
   JPEGs, 10,024,098 bytes; packed with `--compressed-temporary --prioritise-decoding`, 5,403,412
   bytes; the packer's report: "90 archive(s) carried as what they hold, remade exactly on
   installing" and "6 JPEG(s) carried as their brunsli form"): **installed in 6.15 to 6.50 s,
   from 12.93 to 13.85 s** (five runs each, taking turns), every file the same each time.
2. **Mixing's large tables on huge pages (Linux).** Each bit's prediction reads 16 counters and a
   match place scattered over up to 256 MiB at B22, so most reads miss the processor's address
   cache. The tables of 4 MiB or more are now aligned to 2 MiB and offered to the kernel as huge
   pages (`madvise`); the codes are the same (the unit suite's mixing checks, and every SHA-256
   above). The process showed 296,960 kB of huge pages, against none before. 16 MB of enwik8
   decoded at B22 F80 C2: **a median 25.27 s against 26.16 s** over seven runs each, taking turns,
   quicker in six of the seven pairs (about 3%). Not on Windows or macOS.
3. **The x86 stream decoded beside a mixed lzma2 stream.** Where the lzma2 stream is mixed and the
   x86 stream holds programs, the x86 stream is decoded on a thread of its own meanwhile. Checked on
   a v29 folder of five texts and a program: Sieve, sieve-install and the oracle give it back
   exactly. Its time was not measured: the x86 stream is LZMA2, under a second for most folders.

**Looked at and left:**
- The mixed lzma2 stream decoded while the listing's other files are: files taken from that stream
  sit between the queued ones in the listing, and some are remade at once, so overlapping them
  would mean reading the listing in two passes in a frozen reader. Not measured; left.
- A primed model kept for the next primed file: priming costs about 0.1 s for each file or group
  mixed with it, so this would matter only for a `.sieve` of many separately primed pages.
- The rope's decoder was made quicker on 31 October and was not looked at again.
- Mixing a large file in independent blocks, decoded at once, would make a mixed file's install
  scale with the cores, but it is a new format (a later version) and costs some size at each
  block's start.
