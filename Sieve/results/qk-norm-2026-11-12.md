# QK-norm, measured (12 November 2026)

IDEAS §19.119's other half: queries and keys normalised before they meet, against attention scores
held at ±128 in a run without a loss scale (results/training-limits-2026-11-04.md). Measured as
§19.119 asked ("whether the scores then stay inside, and whether the unscaled run learns as the
scaled ones do") before building it as a training version.

## How

A build of Sieve with an experiment switched on by an environment variable (not kept): each head's
query and key put through the norm the layers use (§12.0e, no learnt scale) before the rotary turn,
its gradient through that norm's backward pass. Everything else as `training-v3`.

The run of 4 November: 4 layers, width 64, 4 heads, 8 bits a weight (278,528 weights), the first
60,000 bytes of today's docs/MANUAL.md, 2 epochs (469 steps), each at loss scale 2^0 and 2^6 (the
suggested scale for this shape, and the default; Sieve's default learning rates: 0.0002 and 0.0001),
then trained again with `--limits`. Every run reproduced its result.txt.

| Loss scale | QK-norm | Activations held at ±128 | Gradients held | Weights at an end after | Right byte given (mean of the last 3 checkpoints) |
| ---: | :---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2^0 | no | 17,451,305 of 1,023,310,208 (1.71%) | 151 | 7,363 (2.64%) | 14.61% |
| 2^0 | yes | **0** of 1,084,749,184 | 1 | 2,851 (1.02%) | 14.94% |
| 2^6 | no | 0 | 206,410 (0.015%) | 1,820 (0.65%) | **15.99%** |
| 2^6 | yes | 0 | 230,559 (0.015%) | 1,804 (0.65%) | 15.82% |

The right byte's mean probability at each checkpoint (steps 100 to 469): 8.5, 11.7, 13.9, 14.7,
15.3% without either; 8.9, 12.4, 14.4, 14.8, 15.7% with QK-norm alone; 9.7, 12.9, 14.9, 16.0, 17.2%
with the loss scale alone; 9.6, 12.8, 14.9, 15.9, 16.7% with both.

## What it says

- **QK-norm does what it is for:** without a loss scale, no attention score reached ±128 (from 17.5
  million held), and fewer weights ended at an end of their range.
- **But the loss scale already does it, and learns more.** Every new run of this size is given a
  loss scale (the default), and with it no score was held either way. With the scale, QK-norm learnt
  a little less (15.82% against 15.99%), a difference within what one run of each can show.
- So on what can be measured here, a `training-v4` with QK-norm would change every model's
  arithmetic (the AI line's readers and the hallway's chat would have to follow it) for no gain.
  **Not built.** It is worth measuring again on a model much larger than this one (Edward's own
  training, IDEAS §19.120's "larger model"), where float training meets scores growing without
  bound; a learnt scale on the norm, as float models give it, would also be part of that measure.
