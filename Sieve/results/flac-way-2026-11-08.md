# The FLAC way (v34 draft, 8 November 2026)

Edward (8 November 2026): "make a start on that, use the three levels as the size/balanced/speed
paths for the relevant audio types to go through FLAC". Built as v34 draft's `flac`
(`tools/cli/flac_wav.hpp`, SPECIFICATIONS §12.2 v34): a WAV carried as its samples coded by libFLAC
1.4.3 and the rest of the file as it was, made again byte for byte on installing.

## The three paths

libFLAC's settings, on WoWRevived's Music-Martian/03 (54,987,452 bytes, 16-bit stereo, 44.1 kHz), one
core here (a prototype linking libFLAC, each stream decoded back to the same samples):

| Setting | Bytes | Made in | Decoded in |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| level 5 | 30,660,755 | 0.47 s | 0.28 s |
| level 8 | 30,393,581 | 1.11 s | 0.28 s |
| level 8, coefficients' precision searched | 30,373,205 | 3.92 s | 0.30 s |
| level 8, exhaustive model search | 30,374,747 | 4.11 s | 0.24 s |
| level 8, prediction up to 32 back (out of the subset) | 30,363,966 | 4.92 s | 0.30 s |
| level 8, exhaustive and precision searched | 30,352,432 | 29.63 s | 0.29 s |
| **level 8, 32 back, precision searched** | **30,341,223** | **19.67 s** | **0.27 s** |
| level 8, 32 back, exhaustive | 30,340,684 | 65.89 s | 0.25 s |

(ffmpeg's own FLAC encoder at its level 12 made 30,705,887 of the same file: libFLAC's level 5 is
already smaller.) Taken: **Size**, level 8 with prediction up to 32 back and the precision searched;
**Balanced**, level 8; **Decoding speed**, level 5. Every setting decodes in about the same time, so
the priority here chooses how long making takes. On 8 MB of a 24-bit orchestra (Brahms, Musopen):
4,410,427, 4,408,020 and 4,406,420 bytes; on 8 MB of speech: 2,433,224, 2,397,372 and 2,381,923.

## WoWRevived's seven WAVs

The seven 16-bit stereo WAVs the rope carried in ROPE-POST section 4 (375,901,652 bytes; Music-Human
02 to 05, Music-Martian 03 to 05), each priority made with `--v34-draft --optimise-search`, installed
with `sieve install` and compared with `diff -r`, here:

| Priority | `.sieve` | Made in | Installed in | Given back |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | :--- |
| Size | 222,740,143 | 203.1 s | 7.0 s | every file the same |
| Balanced | 223,209,005 | 51.8 s | 6.9 s | every file the same |
| Decoding speed | 224,634,367 | 48.1 s | 6.6 s | every file the same |

Against the rope's 276,730,651 bytes for the same files (ROPE-POST section 4), Size is 19.5% smaller;
against ffmpeg's FLAC at level 12, 224,783,126, it is 0.9% smaller. The FLAC streams are carried
afterwards as any file is; the search found the rope's plain byte-frequency coding (`L8 W1 S1 P0`, in
pieces) about 0.1% under them.

**Two costs found and cut on the way.** The first runs took 429 s (Size), 271 s (Balanced) and 243 s
(Decoding speed). Of one WAV's 39 s at Decoding speed, 34 s went to LZMA2's estimate of the whole
WAV, made for every file the packer may open, ahead on the threads and again in the loop, and thrown
away when FLAC takes it: now a WAV is weighed first against LZMA2 on 4 MiB taken in eight stretches
from across it, FLAC kept at once where it is under nine tenths of that (here 70% to 84%), and the
whole estimated only where it is a near thing (6.9 s for that WAV). Then Optimise Search turned Try
every way on for each FLAC stream (it is over 16 MiB), searching the rope whole on bytes already
coded: a FLAC stream now keeps the shortcuts. Together, Balanced went from 192 s to 52 s with the
same bytes; Size's remaining time is libFLAC's own hardest setting, about 20 s a file.

## Checked

- **Awkward WAVs** (CI step 11): 8-bit mono of an odd length with a LIST chunk after its data,
  16-bit stereo with a JUNK chunk before its format, 24-bit WAVE_FORMAT_EXTENSIBLE and 32-bit mono,
  all opened; a floating-point WAV and one whose data chunk says more than the file holds, left to the
  other ways; given back by `sieve install`, `sieve-install` and the oracle, which decodes FLAC
  itself (RFC 9639, `flac_decode` in `reference/sieve_ref.py`); Decoding speed too; none opened
  without the draft.
- **A pure synthetic tone** is not taken: on a 24-bit sine with a little noise, LZMA2 came to 59,563
  bytes and FLAC to 64,754 (a tone that repeats exactly is what LZMA2 does best); the weighing chose
  mixing.
- **Damaged streams**: 197,195 mutations of four opened WAVs' FLAC streams and skeletons (bits
  flipped, bytes changed, put in and taken out, the skeleton's size changed), each made again under
  AddressSanitizer and UBSan: 194,023 refused, 3,172 made (a change in a chunk's bytes the skeleton
  carries as they are), none crashing or reading out of bounds. The reader fuzzer
  (`tools/fuzz/fuzz_reader.py`) has `flac` recipes among its ways.

## AIFF (added the same day)

The same recipe, the skeleton's first bytes saying which it is (`RIFF`...`WAVE` or `FORM`...`AIFF`/`AIFC`).
Three AIFFs ffmpeg wrote from the 8 MB pieces (the first seconds): jazz, 16-bit (529,254 bytes:
FLAC stream and skeleton 315,750), the orchestra, 24-bit (576,054: 314,032), and speech as AIFF-C
`sowt` (529,272: 163,685), all opened at Balanced and given back. CI step 11 writes four more by hand
(16-bit stereo with a NAME chunk before COMM, 8-bit mono of an odd length with an ANNO chunk after
its samples, 24-bit mono, AIFF-C `sowt`), all opened, and a float AIFF-C and one with its COMM after
its SSND, left alone; Sieve, sieve-install and the oracle give every one back.

## RF64, Wave64, .au and CAF (added the same day)

The same recipe again. Six files ffmpeg wrote from the jazz and orchestra pieces (3 s and 2 s): CAF
little-endian (529,330 bytes: 316,293 carried), RF64 (529,314: 316,277), `.au` (529,232: 316,195),
Wave64 (529,304: 316,267), CAF big-endian 24-bit (576,130: 314,308) and an `.au` written to a pipe,
its size never written (352,832: 209,508), at Decoding speed, all opened and given back by Sieve,
sieve-install and the oracle. CI step 11 writes six more by hand.

## On several threads (added the same day)

The sound files' FLAC coding now runs before the packer's loop, each file on its own, on every core
(the packer's own run-ahead takes at most 64 MiB at a time, so each of these WAVs had a batch alone),
keeping at most 512 MiB of files in hand. WoWRevived's seven WAVs at Size, here (4 cores): made in
110.5 s, from 203.1 s; the same 222,740,143 bytes, every file given back.

## Sound as FLAC (added the same day)

`--sound-to-flac` (the File Locator's box W, while the draft is on): sound installed as `name.flac`
in FLAC's streamable subset, and FLAC files re-coded. On CI step 11's folder, ten of its eighteen sound
files were installed as `.flac` (907,994 bytes as they were, 539,942 as FLAC), each decoded by the
oracle's own FLAC reader to its file's samples; the rest are float, 32-bit, cut short, COMM-last, or
had a namesake take their `.flac` name first. Two FLACs ffmpeg wrote, re-coded at Size: a jazz piece
at its level 5, 2,144,464 bytes to 2,124,100 (1.0%), its title and artist kept; speech at its level 2
with a cover picture, 1,190,822 to 1,063,449 (10.7%), its picture kept; the same samples decoded by
ffmpeg from both.
