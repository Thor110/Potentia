# A picture of few colours mixed as its palette (2 November 2026)

IDEAS §19.114 (2), from the palette-size filter: a picture of 256 colours or fewer held as one
byte a pixel, an index into its palette, before it is mixed. Measured first, then built in the v33
draft as mix-v4's `P`.

## Measured first

The repository's PNGs of 200 KB of pixels or more holding 256 colours or fewer: 17, all the
hallway's screenshots (1280 by 720, 3 to 21 colours; a narrow set: no sprites, diagrams or pixel
art were at hand). Each written as raw RGB and packed (the packer's defaults, v32), against the
same pixels as one-byte indices with the palette beside them (the palette's bytes counted):

- Indices in the order the colours first appear: smaller for 16 of 17 (by 13% to 48%); one 1.5%
  larger. In order of how often each colour is used: no better overall.
- Against what Sieve does with the PNGs today (Compressed formats temporary: these are
  stb_image_write's PNGs, carried as their pixels and mixed as a picture of their own shape):
  185,088 bytes for the 17 together; the indices 159,087 (−14.0%); the smaller of the two for each,
  155,800 (−15.8%).

## Built (v33 draft)

`P<n>` after a picture's shape in mix-v4's parameters (`mix4 B22 I1280x3 P7`): the pixels mixed as
their indices into a palette of n colours (one byte a pixel, a picture of the same width), then
the palette, n entries of the pixel's bytes, the colours in the order the picture first shows
them, entries past its last colour zero; decoded, the pixels made from them. Only for pixels of 2
to 4 bytes and 1 to 256 colours. The packer weighs it on a picture's start (whole pixels) against
its best without, and keeps the smaller.

And at any version: where the rope's reading of a file is a picture under 16 wide (a wave whose
line is a pixel or two), the shape guessed from the bytes is weighed too (taken only where 2%
better on the start, as before). On the 13 pictures of results/rope-probe-2026-11-02.md it changed
nothing; on six-colour drawings of 320 by 240 that the rope read as 3 wide, 10,944 → 9,133 bytes
and 8,005 → 7,516.
