command line's own default, Optimise Search off) on enwik9's first 1, 4, 16, 64 and 256 MiB and
the whole (954 MiB), each installed again and compared. This container: Linux, 4 logical
processors, 16 GB of memory, so the times are not a desktop's.

## Where it bends

- **Installing** stays fast and small throughout: 16.6 s and 2.7 GB for the whole. Up to 16 MiB the
  file is mixed (slow to install, 18.8 s at 16 MiB); from 64 MiB it is LZMA2 (1.2 s), as mixing over
  16 MiB is not tried without Try every way, and now has to clear the rising bar where it is.
- **Making** is the roof. Its time grows a little faster than the file (about 3.6 s a MiB at 954 MiB,
  against 3.7 at 64), but its memory is what stops it: from 64 MiB it rises by about 11.5 MiB for
  every MiB read, on top of about 2 GB, so the whole of enwik9 took 12.95 GB of this machine's 16.
  From that line (not measured past the whole): about 1.2 GiB of input is the most this machine's
  16 GB could make, and a 32 GB machine's about 2.6 GiB.
- **With Optimise Search** (the File Locator's default), measured at 64 and 256 MiB: 14,472,153 and
  55,977,187 bytes (14% and 13% smaller: mixed), in 290.7 s and 1,269.8 s, at 1,597 and 3,038 MiB
  at the most, far less than without it at 256 MiB.
- **The whole** came to 211,195,707 bytes, 11 more than Edward's machine's LZMA2 figure for it
  (211,195,696, IDEAS 19.113).

## What changed from it

The File Locator's memory estimate (IDEAS 19.97) held with Optimise Search ticked (it says 1,226
and 2,954 MiB at 64 and 256 MiB, measured 1,597 and 3,038), but said 9,236 MiB for the whole with
it unticked, which took 12,950. Unticked and from 64 MiB, it now says 2,000 MiB and 11.5 times
what is read: 2,736, 4,944 and 12,971 against 2,664, 4,950 and 12,950.
