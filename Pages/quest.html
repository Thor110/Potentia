Potentia // System FAQ

Architecture clarifications and stress-tests for the Open Source World Order framework.
Q: How does identity verification avoid capture or fraud?
The same way the current system does, this isn't a replacement, just a stabiliser.
Q: How does weighted voting prevent populism or instant collapse?
Why would you want to prevent populism? Isn't that the point of democracy?
Q: How does the knowledge-validation layer work?
Just as with current voting systems, you would attend a location to vote and would be quizzed on what you are voting for. Ideally this would be something people could do remotely, but there would be no way to stop someone from viewing the publicly accessible database of answers at the same time.
Q: What prevents coordinated manipulation?
Technically nothing, but due to the required time people would have to spend invested in pursuing their personal interest, there would be little time to follow the guidance of others.
Q: What is the failure model?
A failure of this system would likely just result in reverting to the old system of elected representatives.
Q: What happens during transition from the old system?
Our currently elected representatives will be joined by IT professionals in order to translate the matters that they vote on into matters everyone can vote on.
Q: What kind of consensus algorithm applies?
A consensus is almost impossible to achieve when it comes to voting systems.
Q: What role do experts play?
The same as they currently do, educate, discuss, debate.
Q: How is misinformation filtered?
As opposed to how it is or isn't filtered currently? By the collective, misinformation or information would be checked akin to wikipedia.
Q: How is data stored?
The same way it is currently stored, most of the worlds code resides on GitHub, I see that this would or could be too.
Q: How is minority voice protected?
Should minority voice be protected? If the majority say 1+1=2 and a minority says 1+1=3 what do we do? Protect them? Presumably you mean minorities in the ethnic sense, which would be handled by the legal systems currently in place.
Q: What computational limits apply?
The same as current systems, volume, which for the most part has already been overcome.
Q: What prevents voter fatigue?
Nothing, it shouldn't be prevented, people should vote on the matters they are interested in, though realistically, why couldn't people vote on everything? The government already does and there are regular people living regular lives currently that keep up on every decision they make already.
