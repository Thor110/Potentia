# OLAP: The Evolution of the Idea

*A running record of the OLAP Initiative: where it started, how it has changed and why, where it
stands now, and what is still open. When a change or a discovery alters something, it is added
here, and nothing earlier is deleted. Superseded ideas stay, marked as such, so the line of
thought can be reviewed and, if a later finding calls for it, walked back.*

**Concept: Edward James Gordon.** Entries marked *Claude* were worked out with Claude. How the
simulation page works is in [README.md](README.md); the page is
[`Pages/space.html`](../space.html).

## 1. The aim

*(Edward, 6 November 2026)*

To give humanity a guaranteed way of taking **all** of humanity with it, when, or if, we ever
find a way to leave the solar system.

Almost regardless of the approach, the time frame for leaving by ship is too great to take
everyone; the majority would be left behind, unless the population shrank rapidly. The deepest
wish behind the plan is that humanity should never face the fate of fighting to survive while
boiling alive on the surface of a brightening Sun's planet.

## 2. Principles

- **Everyone goes.** No one has to be chosen. *(Edward, the aim above; also OLA Integration:
  even in an evacuation, a large part of the population stays so the platform is never
  abandoned.)*
- **The flywheels are the steering wheel.** They rotate and align the planet, as reaction
  wheels turn a satellite. Nothing is meant to be thrown off; if that ever becomes a necessity,
  it does, and the system stays the steering wheel regardless. *(Edward, 6 November 2026)*
- **Steering first, moving second.** *(Edward, 6 November 2026)*
- **Realistic, manageable and safe.** Train speeds and every other figure must stay within
  what can be built and run safely. Safety is the hardest part to calculate, because it needs
  the full engineering; what cannot yet be counted is said so, not guessed. *(Edward,
  6 November 2026)*
- **Grounded in current physics, not imaginary technology.** *(The OLAP Initiative, p.7)*
- **Reviewable.** Every change is recorded here with its reason, so the ideas can be revisited.
  *(Edward, 6 November 2026)*
- **Start locally.** Recycling at home and on every street adds up to the larger goal: treat the
  planet as a spaceship, not a litter bin. *(The OLAP Initiative, p.2)*

## 3. The evolution, step by step

Each step says what was proposed, and what it changed. Statuses: **current**, **revised**
(changed by a later step, which is named), **superseded** (replaced), **open** (unresolved).

### Step 1 — The OLAP Initiative, v0.1 (Edward, 25 April 2025)

*A Long-Term Sustainability and Escape Architecture for Humanity.*

- **Sustainability first:** a recycling centre on every street, education, and the Earth
  treated as a spaceship. Supports the AIrchive (now Potentia). — *current*
- **A global grid:** 6 quad-stations, 12 dual-stations and 8 tri-stations, joined by mag-lev
  lines; underground cities to house the whole human race; surface access; as many stations on
  land as possible; those at sea built later, once the oceans freeze on the way out.
  — *current* (the 9 great circles of the cube's symmetry, Step 5)
- **Planetary flywheels:** "an inter-connected system of planetary flywheels capable of actively
  altering the planet's axis and therefore altering its orbit", by conservation of angular
  momentum, as satellites are oriented. — *revised* (Steps 5 and 6: the axis yes, the orbit no)
- **The route:** slingshot the planet out of the solar system in 1–2 billion years, before the
  Sun makes it uninhabitable; then round the Galactic Centre towards another galaxy; colonies in
  the Milky Way first, each colony later building its own platform and aiming for another
  galaxy. — *current as the route; the Galactic Centre leg is open* (Step 6)
- **Time to leave:** about 8,000 years to 50,000 AU (50,000 AU divided by the 2π AU the Earth
  travels a year). — *confirmed* (Step 5: holds if the Earth leaves at about its orbital speed)
- **The flywheel proof (GPT-4o's working):** a 0.1% shift of the Earth's spin over a million
  nudges, each needing 5.6×10¹⁹ kg at 1 rpm on a 1 km radius. — *revised* (Step 5: the
  arithmetic is right, but the nudges add up, so the wheels must together hold all of it)
- **Precedent:** the Three Gorges Dam moved the Earth's axis about 2 cm. — *current*, as evidence
  that moving mass on the Earth moves its axis (not its orbit)
- **Addendum, the Oort cloud:** breaking through might do too much damage; alternatives were
  sacrificing the Earth and following with Mars, building deep enough to survive impacts, or
  hoping the cloud has ground itself to dust. — *answered* (Step 5: the cloud is so sparse that
  crossing it adds almost nothing to the risk)
- **Political:** "Where is my %?": every person entitled to a share of the Earth. — *current*

### Step 2 — The companion documents (Edward, 2025)

- **Global Positioning & Alignment Systems:** neutrino emitters and detectors at the bottom of
  each facility, synchronising every node's alignment, so the flywheels transfer momentum in
  the right direction even as the continents drift. — *current, and made necessary* (Step 5:
  the rings must hand momentum between them all day)
- **OLA Integration:** six Orbital Launch Accelerators round each station (156 in all), for
  colonies and evacuations, with care over weaponisation and failed launches. — *current, for
  colonies and payloads* (not as the engine that moves the planet: Step 6)
- **Planetary Standard Kit, The Road to OLAP, the Recycling Facility, the moodboards:** the
  social and practical side: a kit earned by helping make it, a convoy of workshop coaches, a
  headquarters and circular city, compost printing. — *current* (not modelled by the page)
- **Earlier discussions with AI models:** possible, but the scale, the number of passes, the
  number of flywheels, their mass and much more come into question very fast. — *current*, and
  now quantified (Steps 5 to 8)

### Step 4 — The simulation built, v0.2 (Claude, 6 November 2026)

The route played out with measured data: the 2MRS redshift survey, the Local Group, exoplanets,
land outlines, all checked by SHA-256; a Galaxy model; the expanding universe's limit.

- **A misreading, recorded:** to change the orbit, the page offered throwing mass off the Earth
  through the launch accelerators, and assumed it for the Galactic Centre pushes. That came from
  the long history of mass drivers as a way to move bodies, not from Edward's writing.
  — *superseded* (Step 6)

### Step 5 — What the numbers showed (Claude, 6 November 2026)

- **Internal forces cannot move the orbit.** Flywheels, trains, any machinery inside the Earth
  can turn its axis and set its day, but cannot move its centre of mass round the Sun.
- **The proof's nudges add up:** holding the 0.1% shift takes 9.4 Earth masses of 1 km wheels at
  1 rpm. Wider and faster cuts that.
- **The published 7,961 years** to 50,000 AU holds at an exit speed of about 29.8 km/s.
- **The Oort cloud** is about 10⁻⁷ expected collisions to cross: far below ordinary impact risk.
- **The Galactic Centre slingshot works** once there (the Oberth effect: a 30 km/s push at 300 AU
  from Sgr A* leaves the Galaxy at about 110 km/s), but getting there means cancelling 241 km/s.
- **The universe's expansion** limits the Earth, at that speed, to about 0.9 Mpc ever:
  Andromeda, which is coming to us anyway.

### Step 6 — The steering wheel (Edward, 6 November 2026)

- **No mass ejection.** The system steers; if ejection ever becomes necessary it does, but the
  flywheels stay the steering wheel. — *current*
- **More flywheels round each ring,** between the crossings, and perhaps a mag-lev or ordinary
  train acting as the circle itself. — *current, and the best design* (below)
- **Nudges at the Sun's closest pass to take gravitational energy from it.** — *not possible*
  (below), recorded so it can be revisited if new physics changes it
- **Other uses:** if it cannot move a planet, redesign it for smaller bodies, or scale it up as
  part of a Dyson sphere. — *current, open*

*Claude's findings in answer:*

- **The ring is the flywheel; the trains are its rim.** How the mass is split does not change
  the total. Only radius and speed reduce it, and nothing on a planet has a larger radius than
  a ring round all of it.
- **The nudges at the Sun:** the orbit is nearly circular (0.983 to 1.017 AU); the Sun is the
  fixed centre and gives nothing; its pull differs across the Earth by only 2×10⁻⁹ of itself.
  Shifting 0.1% of the Earth's mass every orbit would take about 10¹³ years to escape.
- **The engine without ejection:** asteroid gravity assists, drawing energy from Jupiter (after
  Korycansky, Laughlin & Adams 2001). About 130,000 passes keep the Earth habitable to a billion
  years; escape needs millions more, with several asteroids in turn.
- **The open problem:** the drop to the Galactic Centre. A star passes within 1 AU of the Earth
  about once in 10¹² years, so stellar assists cannot do it.
- **Small bodies:** a 1° tilt of a 10 km asteroid takes trains equal to a steel ring 5.5 m square
  at today's train record: buildable now.
- Page v0.3: steering first; any body; gravity assists the default engine; the Galactic Centre
  drop marked open.

### Step 7 — Everyone, energy and safety (Edward, 6 November 2026)

- **The aim stated:** a guaranteed way to take all of humanity; avoid the fate of the surface.
- **Train speeds realistic, manageable and safe.** — *current*: the page's default is now
  today's record (603 km/h), with a tube at 1,000 km/h, a speculative vacuum tube, and orbital
  speed as the theoretical limit.
- **Safety:** the hardest part to calculate; estimate or leave open, but always consider.
  — *current*
- **Surface maps for the other bodies, as the Earth has.** — *done*
- **Keep this record of the evolution.** — *this document*

### Step 8 — The energy budget, and ships against the planet (Claude, 6 November 2026)

- **Energy alone cannot move a planet without something to push against.** With no mass thrown
  off, the only thing left to throw is light: beaming all the sunlight that reaches the Earth out
  behind it for a billion years changes its speed by 3.1 m/s, against the ~59 km/s an escape
  needs. Fusion, fission and sunlight can run the steering, the cities and the machines; the
  orbit must be changed by a body that moves, such as Jupiter.
- **Every store on the Earth, used whole:**

  | Store | Joules |
  |---|---|
  | All sunlight reaching the Earth for a billion years | 5.5×10³³ |
  | Fusing all the oceans' deuterium (48 trillion tonnes) | 1.7×10³¹ |
  | Fissioning all the crust's uranium and thorium | 1.6×10³¹ |
  | The Earth's internal heat for a billion years | 1.5×10³⁰ |
  | The Earth's whole spin | 2.1×10²⁹ |
  | Jupiter's orbital energy (through assists only) | 1.6×10³⁵ |

  Leaving the Sun needs 2.6 to 5.2×10³³ J (a quick push or a slow spiral): about 3% of
  Jupiter's orbital energy, and 300 times all the fusion energy in the oceans.
- **Ships for everyone:** 8.2 billion people at 100 tonnes each is 8.2×10¹⁴ kg. Sending them out
  of the solar system takes about 2.4×10²³ J: some 20 billion times less energy than moving the
  planet. But at today's launch rate (a few thousand tonnes a year) it would take 270 million
  years; to do it in a century needs 2.7 million times today's rate. Then the nearest star is
  43,000 years away at that speed, in closed ships. This is Edward's point in numbers: by ship,
  most people stay behind. The planet costs vastly more energy, but everyone goes, with the
  biosphere, and with millions of years to adjust the course.
- **Safety, what can be counted** (the published goal, trains at 603 km/h, 1,000 per ring):
  - each train carries about 2 million megatonnes of TNT in motion;
  - handing momentum between the rings through the day, the motors push on the track with about
    19 million tonnes-force on every metre.

  More trains split the first; the second depends only on the goal. What cannot yet be counted:
  what the track, tunnel and rock can bear; safe stopping in a fault; the crust and quakes;
  heat, wear and maintenance; graceful failure.
- Page v0.4: the energy budget; ships against the planet; realistic train speeds and the safety
  figures; surface maps of the Moon (NASA LRO), Mars (USGS Viking), Ceres (NASA Dawn, coloured
  by height), and a seeded asteroid.

## 5. Findings, with numbers

`tools/check.mjs`. The train table, at orbital speed at the surface (the theoretical limit):

| Goal (Earth) | Trains at 7.9 km/s | As solid steel, each of 9 rings |
|---|---|---|
| The published 0.1% shift (0.057°) | 1.2×10²⁰ kg | 6.4 km square |
| Tilt 1° | 2.0×10²¹ kg (1.5× the oceans) | 27 km square |
| Lengthen the day by 1 minute | 8.1×10¹⁹ kg | 5.4 km square |

## 6. Open questions and next steps

- **The Galactic Centre drop** (241 km/s) without an engine: the largest open problem.
  Alternatives: stop at leaving the Sun; ride the Milky Way's meeting with Andromeda.
- **The Moon:** does it come along? It steadies the Earth's tilt, could be steered itself, and
  stays bound in a slow escape.
- **Dyson-scale steering:** the same rings round a star or a ring habitat; formation-keeping
  for a swarm.
- **Steering in transit:** pointing a pole along the direction of travel as a shield (a 90° turn
  needs about two Moons of trains at orbital speed).
- **Safety engineering:** the uncounted items in Step 8, once designs exist.
- **Simulate the escape spiral** step by step, with sunlight falling as it goes.
- **Audit the sources** against the original papers (some values were taken from memory when
  the reference sites were blocked).
- **More data:** Cosmicflows-4 distances; any 2MRS galaxy as a destination; Andromeda's motion;
  close stellar passes from Gaia; a true-colour Ceres mosaic.
- **Presets and reports:** "published", "conservative" and "optimistic" plans; a downloadable
  report with the data hashes.

## 7. How to use this record

- **When something changes,** add a new step at the end of section 3, with the date, who, what
  changed and why; update the statuses in section 4. Do not delete earlier steps.
- **To walk an idea back,** find its step, read why it was revised, and add a new step reversing
  it, with the new reason.
- **The page's settings** can recreate most steps: the link to a plan keeps them. Earlier
  versions of the page are in git, and v0.1 is kept as `Previous/spacev1.html`.
