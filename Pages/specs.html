AI Disclosure
The Sieve of Potentia (State Space : Near Zero) is Edward's (Thor110's) work. He has been building its theory and ideas himself over the past few years, and directs everything in it.
  • Gemini (Google) helped him theorise and conceptualise: many of the concepts and ideas, and parts of this website.
  • Claude (Anthropic) has written much of Sieve's code, its tests and its documentation with him, in long working sessions he reviews and tests.
  • The game's own models (the text, picture and melody models in the hallway) are small models trained by Sieve itself: the text model on a public-domain text, and the picture and melody models on units of Sieve's own lines.
Those happy to use AI-assisted work won't mind; those who prefer otherwise deserve to know. Transparency fosters trust.
Every release Edward makes is measured on his own machine and logged, so the figures can be followed over time on the same hardware. Anyone can add their own machine and builds, and compare like with like.

The Limits Of Physics
A floor, not a forecast: the least energy any computer can spend is the energy to erase one bit, kT ln 2 (Landauer's principle). Below, one erased bit stands for one unit looked at, which flatters every computer there will ever be.

      Add Your Own
      1. Build Sieve (Build or Buy) and run tools\run_tests.bat, or make a release with tools\make_release.bat, which also times the unit suite.
      2. Each passing run writes a record to Sieve/metrics/builds, naming your processor, memory and graphics, never your machine's name.
      3. Describe your machine in Sieve/metrics/machines/YOURNAME-1.txt (copy edward-1.txt), add its name to list.txt, and open a pull request with your records.
      Every machine added makes the figures everyone's: the game's statistics become the sum of all of us.
