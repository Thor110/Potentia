Cosmic Horizon & Reachability Metric
95.4% Unreachable

95.4% of the observable universe is already out of reach forever.

Even a signal sent at the speed of light, today, could never get there: space is expanding faster and faster, and the gap grows faster than light can cross it.

We can still see those galaxies, by light they sent billions of years ago, but nothing we send will ever arrive.

And the reachable part keeps shrinking: every second, tens of thousands more stars slip past the edge.

The Plan, In Brief
The OLAP Initiative (Edward James Gordon, 2025) proposes a global grid of nine great-circle rings meeting at 26 stations (6 quad, 12 dual, 8 tri), each with underground habitats, planetary flywheels and six Orbital Launch Accelerators. Its long goal is to take the Earth itself out of the solar system in one to two billion years, before the brightening Sun makes it uninhabitable; to pass the Oort cloud; to slingshot round the Galactic Centre towards another galaxy; and to leave colonies along the way.
This page plays that route out with the measured numbers: how many flywheels and how much mass, how much energy and for how long, which steps known physics allows, and where the plan has to lean on something else. Every figure is worked out below from sources listed at the bottom, and every setting can be changed. The link to a plan keeps its settings.
